## 3. Desktop findings

- Wrapper width 718px at 1440 (prose column). 31 of 33 tables fit without scrolling. Headers never wrap (`white-space: nowrap`); no header wrapped on any table at either viewport.
- **F06** overflows because its third header is 52 characters and nowrap ("Forfeited value ($30K/yr × 4 yrs, single dependent)"): table 837px, every body cell is one line, so the header sets the width. A classic scrollbar (17px) is visible and content is reachable by scrolling. **L12** overflows by 58px (5 columns, 10 rows, dense cells); same scrollbar.
- No clipping or content loss on screen at 1440px; overflow is always recoverable by scrolling.

## 4. Mobile findings (390px)

- 25 of 33 tables overflow; typically 2 of the columns are visible at scroll 0, 1 for F06 and C01. Columns beyond the edge are visibly cut off mid-word, and a scrollbar thumb appears under the table in the capture. No shadow, gradient, or other scroll cue exists in the stylesheet or computed style.
- No sticky or fixed first column on any table. When scrolled to a later column, the row label (Year, Session, Condition, Class) is out of view. Demonstrated on F10 (only "Consequence" visible; rows are 8–13 lines tall, so rows are told apart by separator lines alone) and S02 (matrix cells read "Satisfied — …" with no row label visible).
- Dense wrapping in narrow columns, no loss: I01 cells reach 27 lines in a 190px column; L13 22 lines; L08 19.
- Content is complete and reachable by scrolling on every table.

## 6. Interaction findings

- Markup is uniform on all 33 tables: `<div class="table-container" tabindex="0">`; no `role`, `aria-label`, `aria-labelledby`, `<caption>`, or `scope` attribute. Every table has `<thead>`.
- Real Tab presses from `#4.1` on FAFSA: one prose link, then wrapper F06 (overflowing, `scrollWidth` 837 vs `clientWidth` 718) with a visible focus ring (2px solid `rgb(46,74,104)`, matches `:focus-visible`).
- From `#4.3`: one prose link, then wrapper F07 (non-overflowing, 718 vs 718): it also takes a Tab stop with the same ring and cannot scroll; the next Tab moves on. No keyboard trap. The 12 FAFSA wrappers are 12 of 124 tabbable items in the prose.
- **Keyboard scrolling is unverified.** Arrow keys left `scrollLeft` at 0 on the overflowing wrapper, but the same harness produced no scroll from PageDown or Space on the plain page, so the pane's synthetic keys do not trigger default scrolling; this is a tool limit, not a site finding.
- No demonstrated navigation or comprehension problem from focusable wrappers. Whether an unnamed focusable region is a problem for assistive technology was not tested.

## 8. Repeated patterns, after the inventory

1. **Clipped print tables** (M1): 4 tables, 2 papers (FAFSA, Chisel); F01 and F08 are the same 5-column content twice. The shared condition is a table whose nowrap header or content is wider than the print column, inside an `overflow-x: auto` wrapper that cannot scroll on paper. The cause at rule level (nowrap headers plus the wrapper's overflow) is inferred from the stylesheet and the clip geometry; it was not isolated by experiment because no CSS change is authorized.
2. **Mobile overflow with row-label loss** (M2/M3): 25 tables, all 5 works. Content is reachable; the association loss depends on which column is the row label.
3. **Page-break behavior** (M6): 9 multi-page tables, consistent header repetition, 4 split rows.

Pixel captures taken: desktop F06; mobile F06, F10 (also scrolled), S02, C02, L12; every paper-PDF table page (full-page views; F10 p51, F12 p54, S01 p3, F02/F03/F04/F05/F07 at reduced scale).

## 9. Evidence gaps

- Keyboard scrolling of an overflowing wrapper: unverified (tool limit, §6).
- Mobile is emulated in a desktop Chromium pane at 390px; real-device scrollbar and touch behavior not observed.
- Tables not pixel-captured on screen are covered by rendered geometry only: desktop for all but F06, mobile for all but F06, F10, S02, C02, L12.
- Not tested: screen-reader announcement of unnamed focusable wrappers, dark theme, widths other than 1440/390, the 1099/1100 shell breakpoint for tables.
- PDFs were viewed through pdf.js, not a print dialog or other viewers.
- Corrections during the audit: first locator ranges for F11, I02, S02 were wrong and were re-derived; one capture taken mid-render was discarded; an apparent empty table shell at the foot of F10's p52 was a misread at low resolution and is not a finding.

## 10. Smallest evidence-supported conclusion

The repeated PDF table-clipping defect (**print clipping of wide or nowrap-header tables**: real information loss in the scholarly PDF, 4 tables across 2 papers) earns a bounded implementation experiment. The print-only remedy (rules that stop the wrapper clipping on paper, without author-facing syntax) remains a **hypothesis**: T18.3 did not experimentally isolate the cause and did not design or test any remedy. The rule-level cause is inferred from the stylesheet and the clip geometry. Everything else is `CURRENT BEHAVIOR ACCEPTABLE` or `REQUIRES AUTHOR INTENT` (row labels on mobile); no new table types, syntax, schema, cards, or escape hatches are earned. Table behavior in HTML otherwise needs no reusable change.
